### Abstract

**[English Abstract]**  
This idea white paper proposes a mutualistic marine infrastructure concept inspired by the hermit crab-sea anemone relationship, establishing a cooperative benefit mechanism between artificial marine structures, mooring longlines, macroalgae (Seaweed Archetype), and benthic bio-communities. The structural network provides seaweeds with an optimal photic depth, settlement substrate, and current circulation, while the seaweed-shellfish ecosystem reciprocates with hydrodynamic damping, sensor protection, water filtration, dissolved oxygen supply, carbon dioxide reduction (Blue Carbon), and biological pest control. Furthermore, it outlines a 4-stage trophic cascade originating from barnacle calcified substrates to juvenile fish refuges, helical longline wrapping, and ecological resilience strategies addressing velocity, temperature, and seasonal fluctuations. This paper aims to record a conceptual direction for active coastal restoration infrastructure coexisting with marine vegetation.

### 1. Problem Statement: Limitations of Conventional Reefs and Dichotomy of Biofouling

* **Static Limitations of Conventional Seaweed Reefs:** Conventional seaweed artificial reefs rely on static rock/concrete structures fixed to the seabed. Consequently, in sea areas with high turbidity or significant tidal ranges, seaweeds struggle to continuously secure the optimal depth and sunlight required for photosynthesis.
* **The Prejudice of Unidirectional Drag Obstacles:** In marine buoys and mooring facilities, seaweeds and biofouling organisms have been treated solely as 'entities to be removed' because they increase hydrodynamic drag, causing structural tilting or chain snapping.
* **Neglect of Horizontal Spaces Between Buoys:** Traditional single-buoy installation methods leave vast water surfaces and mooring line spaces between buoys unused as mere transit lanes, failing to capture opportunities for 3D coastal ecological restoration and environmental improvement.
* **Core Question:** Can we design a mutualistic network infrastructure where structures provide optimal floating habitats for seaweeds and shellfish, while the seaweed-shellfish ecosystem simultaneously provides wave damping, water purification, dissolved oxygen supply, and carbon reduction effects to the structure and surrounding waters?

### 2. Core Concept: Mutualism Mechanism Based on the Hermit Crab-Anemone Model & Ecological Corridors

Forms an eco-engineering mutualism structure where the structure (host/mobile body) and seaweeds/shellfish (attached ecosystem) exchange independent benefits.

* **Benefits Provided by Structures and Networks to the Ecosystem (Hermit Crab Role)**
  * **Optimal Photic Depth Maintenance:** Photic-zone floating structures provide substrates ensuring seaweed spores settle continuously at optimal depths (0–5m) for photosynthesis.
  * **Current Circulation & Nutrient Supply:** Flexible wave movements of buoys driven by waves and wind promote seawater circulation around seaweeds, enhancing nutrient exchange efficiency.
  * **Horizontal Ecological Corridors:** Utilizing mooring lines and aquaculture longlines connecting buoys, transforming single point habitats into tens to hundreds of meters of networked 3D canopies, forming safe horizontal movement paths for juvenile fish.

* **Benefits Provided by the Ecosystem to Structures and Waters (SeaAnemone Role)**
  * **Hydrodynamic Damping:** Seaweed canopies formed on mooring chains and connecting lines induce hydrodynamic drag, mitigating violent heaving and pitching motions of structures caused by waves.
  * **Water Purification:** Shellfish (oysters, mussels) remove fine organic particles via filter feeding, and macroalgae (sea lettuce, wakame) absorb excess nitrogen and phosphorus, creating a dual bio-filter that mitigates coastal eutrophication and red tide risks.
  * **Dissolved Oxygen Supply:** Macroalgae directly release oxygen into water during daytime photosynthesis, elevating surface dissolved oxygen (DO) levels and mitigating summer coastal hypoxia events.
  * **Blue Carbon (CO₂ Reduction):** Absorbing dissolved inorganic carbon (DIC) via photosynthesis, mitigating local ocean acidification and fixing carbon into biomass to contribute to marine blue carbon resource creation.
  * **Biological Pest Control:** Resident juvenile fish forage on parasitic pests (caprellids, amphipods) inhabiting seaweed fronds, preserving seaweed commercial value without toxic chemicals while enhancing juvenile growth rates.

* **Spore Funneling Mechanism**
  * **Top Reflection:** Mushroom microstructures on the Top Zone physically prevent spore capture on upper surfaces.
  * **Bottom Capture:** Spores failing to settle on top slide down wave currents into the micro-roughness pores of the Bottom Zone and mooring chains, naturally maximizing lower settlement rates.

* **Fluid Drag Optimization Structure**
  * **Helical Wrapping Chain Structure:** Mooring lines or seedling cords are wound helically along chain links, dispersing vortex-induced vibrations (VIV) and reducing the projected fluid area to mitigate peak chain loads.
  * **Depth-Differentiated Mass Distribution:** Flexible ulva/wakame are placed in high-velocity surface layers (0–2m), while thick-stemmed laminaria are placed in stable mid-layers (<3m) to induce vertical load balance.

* **Independent Auxiliary Entity Modularization**
  * Bottom Zone and mooring chain assemblies are connected via magnetic/mechanical quick-release clamping, securing modular flexibility for horizontal transfer, harvesting, cleaning, and replacement.

### 3. Trophic Cascade & 5-Tier Vertical Ecosystem

The sub-buoy and longline network utilize barnacle structural archetypes (Barnacle Archetype) as primary foundations to form a 4-stage trophic cascade and 5-tier apartment structure extending to juvenile fish nurseries.

* **4-Stage Trophic Cascade Mechanism Centered on Barnacle Substrates**
  * **Stage 1 (3D Substrate Formation):** Barnacle calcified shells and roughness provide primary micro-refuges for small benthic crustaceans.
  * **Stage 2 (Live Feed Supply):** Resident crustaceans among barnacle shells and barnacle nauplius larvae provide continuous high-protein live feed for juvenile fish.
  * **Stage 3 (Boundary Layer Velocity Reduction):** Barnacle surface roughness forms a micro-boundary layer reducing surface current velocity, conserving energy for weak-swimming juvenile fish.
  * **Stage 4 (Seaweed Holdfast Anchor):** Calcified barnacle roughness firmly anchors macroalgae spore holdfasts, completing large seaweed forests and juvenile nurseries.

* **5-Tier Vertical Ecosystem Configuration**
  * **Tier 1 (Base Substrate Tier):** Sessile organisms like barnacles and mussels settle to form solid biological outer wall foundations (Barnacle Archetype — substrate & boundary layer damping).
  * **Tier 2 (3D Outer Wall Tier):** Shellfish and oyster colonies grow, forming porous 3D structures contributing to water filtration (Oysters/Mussels — filtration & shelter).
  * **Tier 3 (Dynamic Inhabitant Tier):** Benthic small bio-communities inhabit created pores (Crabs/Shrimp — benthic food web formation).
  * **Tier 4 (Vegetation Environment Tier):** Macroalgae spores settle on mooring chains, lower surfaces, and longlines to perform photosynthesis, oxygen supply, and blue carbon fixation (Wakame/Sea lettuce/Laminaria — 3-stage succession vegetative corridors).
  * **Tier 5 (Nursery Tier):** Juvenile fish sanctuaries formed around seaweed forests and bio-clusters avoiding predators (Rockfish/Sea Bream juveniles — predator avoidance & nursery).

### 5. Future Value & Implementation: Dynamic Ocean Adaptation & Realization Records

* **Technical Challenges & Analytical Concepts**
  * **Ecological Resilience Design against Dynamic Marine Environments (Velocity, Waves, Temperature, Seasons):**
    * *Velocity & Wave Fluctuations:* Partial canopy shedding may occur during severe offshore wave impacts, but helical longline wrapping disperses drag to minimize detachment ($F_{drag} = \frac{1}{2} \rho v^2 A C_d$, $C_{d\_helical} \approx 0.7$). Detached organic matter is accepted as natural food web circulation.
    * *Seasonal & Temperature Variations (Whitening Phenomenon):* Natural summer macroalgae decline (whitening/die-off) is embraced as a natural ecological cycle rather than artificially blocked. Tiers 1–3 solid substrates maintain reef shapes stably in summer, while spore funneling autonomously restores Tier 4 vegetation during autumn temperature drops.
    * *Climate Change & Extreme Weather:* Embracing temporary ecosystem resets during extreme typhoons, while securing flexibility as a **Mobile Climate-Adaptive Refuge** capable of modular towing and relocation unlike fixed seabed reefs.
  * **Inverted Buoyancy & Weight Balance Design:** Lower bio-attachment is reinterpreted not as a weight burden but as natural self-ballasting and hydrodynamic damping, while capping maximum attachment to prevent sinking: `Max Anticipated Bottom Attached Weight ≤ 30% of Total Reserve Buoyancy` ($W_{bio\_max} \le 0.3 \times B_{reserve}$).
  * **Ecological Light Pollution Minimization (Nocturnal Darkness Protection):** Top Zone LEDs aim for Amber Modes (590–620nm) to protect nocturnal circadian rhythms of Tiers 4–5. Principles include satisfying IALA minimum visibility, Full-cutoff shielding to prevent underwater light leakage, and intermittent flashing (1 flash per 10-sec cycle) guaranteeing at least 9 seconds of darkness.

* **Future Value as a White Paper**
  * This white paper records the paradigm shift where artificial marine structures actively improve coastal environments (water quality, oxygen, carbon) through mutualistic exchanges with vegetation and fish, extending beyond mere buoys or aquaculture facilities.

### 7. Closing Remarks: Toward Coexistence of Structures and Vegetation

The seaweed-structure mutualism platform proposed here is not engineeringly flawless. Severe typhoons may detach canopies, or unforeseen drag may tension mooring lines.
